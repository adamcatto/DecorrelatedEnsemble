\section{Limitations}
This draft does not establish a novel algorithm or strong benchmark performance.
OOF selection risks overfitting to a reused training prediction matrix. Refitting
changes the fitted functions and may change their error dependence. IID row
bootstrap screens do not justify guarantees under overlapping training folds,
large candidate libraries, grouped observations, or temporal shifts. Sparse
shallow trees can fail on interactions that need joint feature access. Only two
generation seeds and one dimensional/sample-size setting have been tested in the
completed studies. Static baselines lack tuning and resources are unmatched. Partial
screening feasibility changes the support of comparisons; aggregate effects can
be driven by one mechanism. These restrictions preclude real-world or broad
algorithm-superiority claims.

\section{Conclusion}
The first development study does not establish a broadly competitive method.
It reveals weaknesses in unadjusted bootstrap screening and in sparse-subspace
aggregation, while co-error offers only modest and conditional gains over quality
selection. Fixed-subset affine calibration helps some regimes, but profiled
selection usually worsens test loss despite improving OOF fit. Follow-up tests
isolate selection uncertainty and candidate-search effects before any locked
benchmark evaluation.
