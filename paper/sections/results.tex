\section{Synthetic Experiments}
\input{tables/exp_001_mechanisms_v1_facts}
Experiment 001 evaluated nine known mechanisms for each of binary classification
and regression: additive, redundant, correlated blocks, sparse, dominant, XOR,
four-way parity, mixed, and null. Fixed settings were $N=600$, $M=40$, $B=100$,
$K=8$, feature fraction $0.1$, tree depth 3, minimum leaf size 5, two generation
seeds, three outer folds, and three OOF folds. This produced
\ExpOneOuterFolds{} paired outer evaluations. Forest and boosting baselines use
static configurations; search compute, final capacity, and inference budgets
are measured but unmatched.

The initial hypothesis is not supported as a broad performance claim at these
settings. Unfiltered co-error improves mean binary AUROC over unfiltered top
quality by \ExpOneAucGain{}, but generally trails the strong forests and boosting
models. Co-error does not consistently improve over absolute residual correlation
on outer AUROC or squared loss; a positive aggregate Brier effect is concentrated
in the dominant-feature regime. The $\alpha=0.5$ shrinkage variant fails to improve
on unregularized co-error in the compared task means. These are negative findings
for the preregistered settings, not universal statements about the objectives.

\subsection{Screening Failure and Feasibility}
Screened co-error is feasible in only \ExpOneBinaryFeasible{} of 54 binary splits
and \ExpOneRegressionFeasible{} of 54 regression splits. On every binary null
split, \ExpOneNullMin{}--\ExpOneNullMax{} of 100 candidates pass the empirical
screen, despite population AUROC $0.5$ under this data-generating process.
The bootstrap lower quantile must therefore remain labeled an empirical screen.
Across shared feasible splits, top-quality screening has almost no incremental
benefit over unscreened top quality; it frequently prevents a fixed-size ensemble.

\subsection{Experiment 002: Attenuation Versus Selection}
\input{tables/exp_002_affine_v1_facts}
The follow-up fixes the candidate pools, masks, seeds, splits, and $B/K$ settings
from Experiment 001. It compares raw versus OOF-affine-calibrated random,
top-quality, and co-error subsets, plus APCE, on six regression mechanisms.
All subsets are unscreened to remove infeasibility as a confound. Each affine
control selects exactly the same learners as its raw counterpart. No aggregation
hyperparameter is tuned; these are two learned scalar parameters.
\input{tables/exp_002_affine_v1_regression}

Affine calibration of co-error improves task-mean normalized loss in
\ExpTwoCalibratedWins{} of six regimes. Additive loss changes from
\ExpTwoAdditiveRaw{} to \ExpTwoAdditiveAffine{}, and mixed loss from
\ExpTwoMixedRaw{} to \ExpTwoMixedAffine{}. These improvements leave a large gap
to the unrestricted forest on additive signal (\ExpTwoAdditiveForest{}).
The null control worsens from \ExpTwoNullRaw{} to \ExpTwoNullAffine{}.
Calibration therefore addresses part of the weakness without establishing a
broadly competitive method or a unique attenuation explanation.

APCE improves or ties calibrated OOF loss relative to affine co-error in all
\ExpTwoOOFNoWorse{} splits, yet loses on outer normalized loss in
\ExpTwoProfiledLosses{} of six task means and wins only the redundant regime.
On additive signal its outer loss is \ExpTwoAdditiveProfiled{}, versus
\ExpTwoAdditiveAffine{} for calibration of the unchanged co-error subset.
The additional optimization improves the reused training criterion while usually
hurting generalization. This is consistent with selection optimism; smaller
training fits for OOF predictions and the subsequent refit remain alternatives.

\subsection{Experiment 003: Candidate Search at Fixed Ensemble Size}
\input{tables/exp_003_pool_scaling_v1_facts}
We vary $B\in\{30,100,300\}$ at fixed $K=8$ on additive and null mechanisms
for both tasks, holding all other settings fixed. Across the 48 adjacent-pool
fold comparisons, masks, OOF predictions, and bootstrap columns agree exactly
on their prefixes; the fixed forest also gives identical predictions. Larger
pools improve binary additive co-error performance, while additive regression
is non-monotone: normalized co-error loss is
\ExpThreeAdditiveCoerrorBThirty{}, \ExpThreeAdditiveCoerrorBHundred{}, and
\ExpThreeAdditiveCoerrorBThreeHundred{}, respectively.

The flexible profiled selector exposes increasing optimism. Its additive
training OOF MSE decreases from \ExpThreeAdditiveProfiledOOFBThirty{} through
\ExpThreeAdditiveProfiledOOFBHundred{} to \ExpThreeAdditiveProfiledOOFBThreeHundred{},
while outer normalized loss changes from \ExpThreeAdditiveProfiledBThirty{} to
\ExpThreeAdditiveProfiledBHundred{} and \ExpThreeAdditiveProfiledBThreeHundred{}.
The endpoint deterioration occurs for both generation seeds in additive and null
regression. Fixed $\alpha=0.5$ shrinkage supplies no consistent outer remedy.
Thus a larger library improves the reused criterion without a monotone
generalization benefit for these selectors.

Binary null survivors rise from \ExpThreeNullBinaryBThirtyMin{}--\ExpThreeNullBinaryBThirtyMax{}
at $B=30$ to \ExpThreeNullBinaryBThreeHundredMin{}--\ExpThreeNullBinaryBThreeHundredMax{}
at $B=300$. Regression null screening retains no candidates at any pool size.
This separates multiplicity-related false survival from the regression screen's
low feasibility/power. It does not estimate familywise coverage from six splits.
All comparisons reuse development seeds and remain descriptive.

\subsection{Experiment 004: Three Real Development Tasks}
\input{tables/exp_004_real_v1_facts}
The two preregistered feature-width runs complete all 36 outer evaluations;
the paired-control audit verifies identical splits and unchanged baseline
predictions. All task-applicable screened subsets are feasible. Empirical
top-quality screening chooses the exact same subset and weights as unscreened
top quality in all \ExpFourSameTopSubsets{} paired cases. This panel supplies
no evidence of incremental benefit from that screen for top-quality selection.
\input{tables/exp_004_real_v1_results}

Unfiltered co-error improves primary task means relative to unfiltered top
quality on all three datasets at both widths, but trails the fixed Random Forest
reference in each case. The simple logistic/ridge control performs better still:
breast-cancer AUROC is \ExpFourCancerLinearNarrow{}, wine log loss
\ExpFourWineLinearNarrow{}, and diabetes RMSE \ExpFourDiabetesLinearNarrow{}.
The full table preserves stronger and weaker static baselines; this is not a
tuned or resource-matched superiority comparison.

Increasing width changes wine co-error log loss from
\ExpFourWineCoerrorNarrow{} to \ExpFourWineCoerrorWide{}, approaching the forest
(\ExpFourWineForestNarrow{}) but remaining worse than logistic regression.
Diabetes RMSE changes from \ExpFourDiabetesCoerrorNarrow{} to
\ExpFourDiabetesCoerrorWide{}, while the forest achieves
\ExpFourDiabetesForestNarrow{}. Breast-cancer AUROC worsens from
\ExpFourCancerCoerrorNarrow{} to \ExpFourCancerCoerrorWide{}.
Wider masks are therefore not a universal remedy. Wine's top-quality log loss
worsens with width; stored probability-tail diagnostics reveal true-class zeros
in three of six wide top-quality splits, making clipped log loss sensitive to
those failures. Quality screening by macro AUROC does not control this behavior.

An exploratory duplicate-column audit exposes another explanation for selection
gains. The diabetes narrow pool has exactly \ExpFourDiabetesPoolDistinctMin{}
distinct OOF prediction columns despite $B=100$. Its eight top-quality
specifications represent only \ExpFourDiabetesTopDistinctMin{}--\ExpFourDiabetesTopDistinctMax{}
distinct observed prediction vectors; co-error represents
\ExpFourDiabetesCoerrorDistinctMin{}--\ExpFourDiabetesCoerrorDistinctMax{}.
Equal weights over distinct specifications can thus produce unequal effective
weights over repeated prediction vectors. OOF equality is not global functional
identity and does not guarantee identical refits. This confound prevents
attributing the quality-control gap solely to independent complementary learners.
The follow-up ablation removes exact OOF duplicates before selection while retaining
fixed $K$, comparing top quality, random, and co-error on the same eligible pool.

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/exp_004_real_v1/performance_vs_fraction.pdf}
\caption{Real development feature-width comparison. Lines are task means and
dots are overlapping outer split observations, not confidence intervals. The
three primary metrics retain their own scales. Linear controls outperform these

The selected tree ensembles are smaller in serialized bytes than the 256-tree
forest, but require more charged search CPU time. Pipeline/preprocessing overhead
also limits inference savings relative to tree-count differences. Stored resource
measurements and the compute--performance figure preserve these tradeoffs; no
isolated-process memory or matched-budget advantage is established.

\subsection{Experiment 005: Duplicate Avoidance Versus Implicit Weighting}
\input{tables/exp_005_oof_duplicates_v1_facts}
All 18 follow-up folds reuse the narrow Experiment 004 pools exactly; the audit
verifies identical raw data, specifications, splits, OOF and bootstrap arrays,
and 108 unchanged-control prediction pairs. All 54 OOF-unique subsets contain
eight exact-distinct training prediction vectors with equal weights. Screening
is removed as a confound and all applicable variants are feasible.
\input{tables/exp_005_oof_duplicates_v1_results}

On diabetes, deduplication worsens co-error RMSE from
\ExpFiveDiabetesCoerrorOriginal{} to \ExpFiveDiabetesCoerrorUnique{}; unique
top quality achieves \ExpFiveDiabetesTopUnique{}. The original co-error advantage
over quality disappears while both unique methods remain weak. Removing
duplicate concentration is therefore not a remedy: forcing eight observed
patterns prevents concentrating weight on a few useful univariate predictors.
The original co-error closely matches the frequency-weighted Caruana reference.
This supports implicit weighting as an explanation for much of the diabetes
quality-control gap, rather than eight distinct complementary specialists.
It does not identify a unique causal mechanism beyond the intervention, because
representative/refit changes and the hard uniqueness constraint alter the method.

Wine's unique top-quality and co-error log losses are both approximately
\ExpFiveWineCoerrorUnique{}, versus \ExpFiveWineTopOriginal{} and
\ExpFiveWineCoerrorOriginal{} originally. The primary-metric gap nearly vanishes;
a modest Brier advantage remains in the stored secondary metrics. Cancer top
quality and co-error are unchanged in the reported primary and Brier split
effects, consistent with its already mostly unique pool. Thus exact duplicate
structure changes the interpretation of selection gains in some regimes, but
cannot explain every gain. No significance claim follows from these three
reused development tasks.

discrepancy below $2.9\times10^{-13}$. All 52 selection settings and nine static

\ExpSixCreditDefaultCo{}, exceeding top quality

\subsection{Molecule-Level Counterexample and Pooling Control}
Musk co-error molecule AUROC is \ExpSixMuskTwoCo{}, below top quality
(\ExpSixMuskTwoTop{}), the forest (\ExpSixMuskTwoRF{}) and all boosting references.
Row Brier nevertheless improves from $.131$ for quality to $.123$ for co-error;
Caruana reaches $.115$ and molecule AUROC \ExpSixMuskTwoCaruana{}.
Thus a row squared-loss improvement need not transfer to a nonlinear molecule
ranking metric. The count-only control averages molecule AUROC $.454$, with
fold values $.498/.456/.408$. It does not support a simple count-only explanation,
but cannot exclude interactions between score distributions, cardinality and max
pooling. There are only 102 independent-molecule units under the assumed sampling
model, not 6,598 independent conformations. This is a negative primary result.

On all nine original classification folds, screening leaves both co-error and

residual-correlation selectors choose the same subsets on all 18 panel folds;

outputs. On HIGGS and MiniBooNE correlation selection improves AUROC relative to

approximately 3,650--6,000 distinct OOF patterns; specification count is not a
count of statistically independent learners. Musk screening survival also varies
sharply across folds (1,372--5,235 of 6,000), reinforcing the sampling-unit caveat.

\section{Ablations and Analysis}
The stored ablations include all required v0 components, signed versus absolute
residual correlation, prediction correlation, covariance without bias, direct
squared-loss greedy selection, with-replacement selection, and shrinkage. An
artifact audit reconstructed loss and test metrics for all selected methods;
the maximum squared-loss/co-error identity discrepancy was below $10^{-10}$,
including the larger-scale raw diabetes targets.
Direct squared-loss and co-error greedy validation objectives agree, confirming
that their distinction supplies no additional mechanism.

\section{Analysis and Next Discriminating Test}
OOF/test loss gaps combine selection, training-size, refit and sampling effects;
they do not uniquely identify optimism. Independent selection holdouts must
compare retained fitted functions with refitted specifications. A conservative
bounded-differences bound can simultaneously certify binary AUROC for a fixed
independent library \citep{mcdiarmid1989}, with no automatic guarantee for OOF
scores or refits. The duplicate-control result motivates comparing regularized
simplex weights on canonical prediction patterns with multiplicity-constrained
discrete weights. Additive synthetic linear/oracle controls remain necessary
to separate representation mismatch from selection failure. These tests precede
tuned, resource-matched confirmation.
