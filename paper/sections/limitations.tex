\section{Limitations}
This draft does not establish a novel algorithm or strong benchmark performance.
OOF selection risks overfitting to a reused training prediction matrix. Refitting
changes the fitted functions and may change their error dependence. IID row
bootstrap screens do not justify guarantees under overlapping training folds,
large candidate libraries, grouped observations, or temporal shifts. Sparse
shallow trees can fail on interactions that need joint feature access. Only two
generation seeds and one dimensional/sample-size setting have been tested in the
completed synthetic studies. The three real tasks are small numerical development
datasets, with only one dataset per task type and repeated splits of the same rows.
Duplicate OOF columns can make equal specification weights into effective frequency
weights, and flattened multiclass correlation includes classwise bias. Static
baselines lack tuning and resources are unmatched.
Phase RSS includes the runner's retained models and shared arrays, so it does not
estimate isolated model memory. Synthetic regimes can share generation randomness;
descriptive task resampling does not supply calibrated interval coverage.
Linear controls have been tested on the real panel; additive synthetic linear/oracle
controls and competitive tuned modern methods remain untested.
Partial screening feasibility changes the support of comparisons; aggregate effects can
be driven by one mechanism. These restrictions preclude real-world or broad
algorithm-superiority claims.

\section{Conclusion}
The first development study does not establish a broadly competitive method.
It reveals weaknesses in unadjusted bootstrap screening and in sparse-subspace
aggregation, while co-error offers only modest and conditional gains over quality
selection. Fixed-subset affine calibration helps some regimes, but profiled
selection usually worsens test loss despite improving OOF fit. Increasing the
candidate library has conditional benefits and can amplify this optimism at
fixed retained size. Independent selection/refit controls remain necessary before
any locked benchmark evaluation.
The real development panel confirms conditional selection gains but also exposes
duplicate-column weighting and a gap to linear models. These findings motivate
mechanism controls rather than a general tabular-performance claim.
